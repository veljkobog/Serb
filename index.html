Straight Talk: How This Works

What the heck is EBITDA?

It stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization. Big words for a simple idea: how much money the business actually makes from doing the work, before the accountant and the bank take their cut. It's the number buyers care about because it strips out things that change from owner to owner.

Why "add back" my own pay?

When someone buys your shop, they're buying the profit after paying a manager to run it — not after paying you specifically. So the salary and perks you take out get added back to show the true earning power. Same goes for one-time costs that a new owner will never see again.

Where does the multiple come from?

Home services businesses typically sell for somewhere between 2.5× and 5× adjusted EBITDA. Recurring maintenance contracts, a strong brand, trained crews that don't need you day-to-day, and clean books push it higher. Heavy owner-dependence, choppy revenue, or a single big customer push it lower. Pest control and HVAC with service agreements tend to top the range; one-truck operations sit at the bottom.

What makes MY number go up?

Recurring revenue (memberships, maintenance plans), a book of repeat customers, documented systems, a management layer so the business runs without you, well-maintained trucks and equipment, and two-plus years of clean financials. Fix those before you sell and you can move the multiple — and the price — meaningfully.
